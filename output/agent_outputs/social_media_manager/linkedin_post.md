## Topic: The Shift from Functional Lighting to Ambient Wellness in Home Design

---

For decades, home lighting was a binary decision: on or off. A fixture's job was to illuminate a room so you could see. Brightness was the only KPI.

That model is breaking. And the numbers tell a clear story.

The global smart lighting market is projected to reach $44 billion by 2028, growing at a CAGR of roughly 20%. But the more interesting shift is about *why* people are adopting — not market size. Consumer surveys show the primary driver is no longer energy savings or convenience. It is atmosphere. Mood. Wellbeing.

Lighting is leaving the utility category and entering the wellness conversation — right alongside sleep, mental health, and home environment design. The pandemic-era home nesting boom accelerated the trend, but it has long outlasted it. Architectural Digest, Dwell, and the Journal of Interior Design have all published pieces in the last eighteen months framing light as an emotional design material, not merely a functional afterthought.

This re-framing matters for anyone in product design, smart home, or DTC hardware. A smart bulb with 16 million colors means nothing if the user cannot translate that into how their living room *feels* at 8 PM on a Tuesday. The companies winning in this space close the gap between technical capability and emotional experience — making ambient design accessible without a lighting designer on speed dial.

TechGlow has been building with this philosophy from day one: premium ambient lighting that works out of the box, without a manual, a hub, or a learning curve. The technology should disappear. The feeling should stay.

Curious how others in product, design, and smart home are thinking about this:

- Is "lighting as wellness" a lasting category shift, or still a premium niche?
- How do you balance feature depth with frictionless setup in hardware products?
- What room in the home is most underserved by current lighting design?

Would value perspectives from across the aisle — designers, engineers, product leads, and anyone who really cares about how their space feels.
